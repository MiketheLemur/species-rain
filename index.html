    What is this

    In 1963, Robert MacArthur and Edward O. Wilson proposed that the number of species on an island settles at an equilibrium: new species immigrate at a rate that falls as the island fills up, while resident species go extinct at a rate that rises as competition tightens. Big islands hold more. Near islands fill faster. Nothing is permanent; the count is stable but the cast keeps turning over.

    This machine runs that model live. Dots drift off the mainland — every one a would-be colonist. Those that reach the island take root and jitter with nervous energy. Every tick, each resident rolls the extinction dice, and the dice are loaded by crowding. Push the sliders: make the island a continent's shy cousin or a rock that only storms can find. Hit Summon storm to throw a burst of colonists at the shore and watch the ledger fill with arrivals the island cannot keep.

    The dashed line on the chart is the model's own prediction of where richness should settle. The solid line is what actually happens. The gap between them is where the interesting stuff lives.
